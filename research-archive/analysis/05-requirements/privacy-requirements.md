# Privacy and Data Protection Requirements in Scam Defense

## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemic Protocol

Scam defense systems inherently process highly sensitive data: personal bank account ledgers, real-time geolocation, interaction cadences, device identifiers, and social communication states. However, in the pursuit of security, systems cannot operate as unfettered surveillance tools. A defense system that violates consumer privacy statutes is legally non-viable and will face regulatory prohibition, civil fines, and consumer rejection.

In strict compliance with Part 13 of the Phase 5 mandate, this document defines the **privacy and data protection requirements** of the system. It grounds every requirement in recognized international privacy legislation—including the European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the Indian Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDP Act 2023), the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), and the US Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA)—distinguishing statutory legal mandates from prudent engineering practices.

## 2. Privacy Engineering Principles in Scam Defense

```text
                     THE PRIVACY-BY-DESIGN FRAMEWORK
                     
  [Principle 1: Strict Data Minimization]
  Only collect telemetry directly correlated with scam detection; zero extraneous harvesting.
  
  [Principle 2: Purpose Limitation & Firewalls]
  Fraud telemetry strictly firewalled from commercial marketing, ad-tech, and credit scoring.
  
  [Principle 3: Ephemeral Processing]
  Raw high-frequency sensor streams processed in volatile RAM; dropped post-settlement.
  
  [Principle 4: Pseudonymization & Cryptographic Sharing]
  Cross-bank queries utilize cryptographic hashes / zero-knowledge proofs; zero plain PII.
```

## 3. Detailed Privacy Requirement Specifications

### 3.1 REQ-PRIV-001: Strict Statutory Purpose Limitation and Commercial Firewalls
- **Statement**: All telemetry, interaction data, behavioral biometrics, and threat indicators collected or generated by the system MUST be used exclusively for fraud, scam, and financial crime prevention, and MUST be technically and operationally firewalled from: (a) commercial ad-targeting, (b) marketing personalization, (c) credit underwriting, and (d) third-party data broker monetization.
- **Rationale**: Statutory mandate. Repurposing fraud security data for commercial marketing violates GDPR Article 5(1)(b) (Purpose Limitation), India DPDP Act 2023 Section 4, and US Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act privacy provisions.
- **Traceability Link**: Dimension C & E (Information Gaps); Statutory Privacy Law.
- **Priority**: **MUST**.
- **Measurable Acceptance Condition**: 100% of fraud data repositories enforce cryptographic access control policies and logical network isolation that prevent access by commercial banking analytics, ad-tech, or external marketing systems.
- **Dependencies**: Database access control policies; data classification tagging.
- **Epistemic Uncertainty**: None; absolute statutory legal requirement.

---

### 3.2 REQ-PRIV-002: Client-Side Ephemeral Processing of High-Frequency Sensor Telemetry
- **Statement**: Raw, continuous client sensor telemetry (such as raw touch coordinate streams, accelerometer readings, and gyroscope displacement vectors) MUST be processed ephemerally in volatile memory on the local device, extracting compact mathematical feature vectors and immediately purging raw sensor data upon session termination.
- **Rationale**: Storing raw continuous physical motion data on persistent cloud servers constitutes massive, unnecessary surveillance, exposing financial institutions to severe data breach liabilities under GDPR Article 5(1)(c) (Data Minimization).
- **Traceability Link**: VG-05 (Communicative Silo), Dimension D (Behavioral Dynamics); EDPB Guidelines 3/2020.
- **Priority**: **MUST**.
- **Measurable Acceptance Condition**: The client SDK retains raw sensor coordinates only in volatile RAM buffers during active typing/swiping; zero raw sensor stream packets are written to persistent device disk or transmitted uncompressed to remote cloud databases.
- **Dependencies**: Client-side feature extraction runtime.
- **Epistemic Uncertainty**: Minimum feature vector representation required without losing diagnostic precision.

---

### 3.3 REQ-PRIV-003: Special Category Biometric Data Compliance (GDPR Article 9)
- **Statement**: If behavioral biometrics (touch dynamics, keystroke cadence) are utilized, the system MUST establish a documented statutory legal basis (such as Substantial Public Interest under GDPR Article 9(2)(g) or explicit consent where legally mandated) and provide consumers with transparent statutory privacy disclosures detailing how behavioral data is processed and protected.
- **Rationale**: Behavioral biometric data used for unique authentication or psychological profiling is legally classified as "special category data" in Europe and other major privacy jurisdictions. Processing without explicit statutory alignment triggers immediate regulatory sanctions.
- **Traceability Link**: VG-08 (Model Governance), Dimension D (Behavioral Privacy); GDPR Article 9.
- **Priority**: **MUST**.
- **Measurable Acceptance Condition**: The system’s Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) receives formal approval from institutional Data Protection Officers (DPOs), confirming compliant legal basis documentation for behavioral feature extraction.
- **Dependencies**: Legal DPIA sign-off; privacy disclosure management.
- **Epistemic Uncertainty**: Divergence in national enforcement attitudes regarding whether behavioral friction constitutes biometric profiling.

---

### 3.4 REQ-PRIV-004: Privacy-Preserving Inter-Bank Entity Querying
- **Statement**: Cross-institutional risk queries and beneficiary risk verifications between competing financial institutions MUST be executed using privacy-preserving cryptographic techniques (such as cryptographic tokenization, Private Set Intersection - PSI, or Secure Multi-Party Computation - SMPC), ensuring that no raw customer names, account numbers, or balances are exposed across institutional boundaries.
- **Rationale**: Traditional banking secrecy laws and cross-border data transfer restrictions legally prohibit Bank B from sharing unmasked customer identity data with Bank A without prior customer consent or formal police subpoenas.
- **Traceability Link**: VG-03 (Bilateral Inter-Bank Asymmetry), Dimension K; Phase 3 Independent Discoveries (PSI / Cryptographic Consortiums).
- **Priority**: **MUST**.
- **Measurable Acceptance Condition**: Inter-bank risk exchange protocols exchange strictly cryptographically blinded tokens or homomorphic risk scores; zero plain-text customer PII (Personally Identifiable Information) crosses institutional network boundaries during real-time queries.
- **Dependencies**: Standardized cryptographic protocol implementation (e.g., PSI / SMPC libraries).
- **Epistemic Uncertainty**: Computational latency of zero-knowledge or SMPC protocols under sub-50ms switch budgets (K-GAP-04).

---

### 3.5 REQ-PRIV-005: Data Retention Limits and Right-to-Erasure Boundaries
- **Statement**: The system MUST enforce automated data lifecycle retention schedules that: (a) purge routine, unflagged transaction telemetry within 90 days of execution, while (b) preserving decision logs for transactions associated with confirmed scam investigations for the statutory regulatory audit window (typically 5 to 7 years under AML retention statutes), ensuring a defensible balance between consumer privacy and financial crime legal compliance.
- **Rationale**: Reconciles the fundamental conflict between data protection laws (GDPR Article 17 - Right to Erasure) and anti-money laundering statutory retention mandates (e.g., US BSA 31 CFR 1010.410, UK Money Laundering Regulations).
- **Traceability Link**: Dimension C & E (Information Gaps); Basel AML Guidelines.
- **Priority**: **MUST**.
- **Measurable Acceptance Condition**: Automated database retention policies automatically delete unflagged interaction logs older than 90 days, while retaining flagged investigation records under tamper-evident archiving for exactly the statutory 5-year compliance period.
- **Dependencies**: Automated data retention and purging schedulers.
- **Epistemic Uncertainty**: Variation in statutory retention periods across international banking jurisdictions (e.g., 5 years in US/UK vs. 10 years in Germany).

---

### 3.6 REQ-PRIV-006: Algorithmic Transparency and Data Subject Access (GDPR Article 15 & 22)
- **Statement**: The system MUST maintain data subject access logging capabilities that enable financial institutions to provide consumers, upon formal request, with a transparent copy of the personal data and behavioral attributes evaluated during a contested transaction decision, in full compliance with statutory data subject access rights.
- **Rationale**: Statutory mandate under GDPR Article 15 (Right of Access) and CCPA. A consumer has the legal right to inspect the data an institution used to evaluate their behavior.
- **Traceability Link**: VG-08 (Model Governance & ECOA Barrier), REQ-EXP-004; CFPB Circular 2022-03.
- **Priority**: **SHOULD**.
- **Measurable Acceptance Condition**: System provides an administrative export API capable of generating a standardized, machine-readable personal data report for a specific customer identifier within 72 hours of an authorized Data Subject Access Request (DSAR).
- **Dependencies**: Customer data indexing and retrieval pipelines.
- **Epistemic Uncertainty**: Redacting proprietary model weights from consumer-facing DSAR exports without violating intellectual property protections.
